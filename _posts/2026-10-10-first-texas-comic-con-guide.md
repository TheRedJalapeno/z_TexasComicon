Going to your first convention is exciting and a little overwhelming. Here's what veteran Texas con-goers wish they'd known before their first one.

## Pick the right first con

Not every con is the same experience.

- **Big multi-fandom shows** like [FAN EXPO Dallas]({{ '/conventions/fan-expo-dallas/' | relative_url }}) and [Comicpalooza]({{ '/conventions/comicpalooza/' | relative_url }}) are about celebrity guests, huge show floors, and spectacle. They're great, but crowded.
- **Community-sized shows** like [Hill Country Comicon]({{ '/conventions/hill-country-comicon/' | relative_url }}), [Bell County Comic Con]({{ '/conventions/bell-county-comic-con/' | relative_url }}), or [AggieCon]({{ '/conventions/aggiecon/' | relative_url }}) are cheaper, calmer, and easier with kids.
- **Hotel-style anime cons** like [Anime North Texas]({{ '/conventions/anime-north-texas/' | relative_url }}) are social and run late into the night. The fun is in the hallways as much as the panels.

## Badges and add-ons

- **Buy early.** Prices usually climb as the show approaches, and single-day Saturday badges at big shows can sell out.
- **Autographs and photo ops cost extra.** At celebrity-driven shows, these add-ons often cost more than admission. Budget for them separately and buy as soon as your must-see guest is announced.
- **Buy only from the official site** or its named ticketing partner. Resold badges can be voided, and organizers increasingly tie badges to names.

## Getting there

- **Downtown venues** (Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, El Paso) have expensive event parking. Dallas's Kay Bailey Hutchison Convention Center has its own DART light rail station, and Houston's George R. Brown is on METRORail.
- **Book hotels the day the room block opens** for San Japan, QuakeCon, and Ikkicon especially. Attached and resort hotels go fast.

## Texas heat is a real factor

Summer cons (Anime Matsuri, Dream Con, QuakeCon, Classic Game Fest, San Japan) happen in triple-digit heat.

- Carry a refillable water bottle. Most venues have filling stations.
- If you're cosplaying, plan the walk from parking to the door. Armor and wigs in 100°F sun is no joke.
- Shade and air-conditioned breaks are part of the schedule, not a waste of time.

## Budget

Beyond badges and add-ons, plan for:

- **Artist alley:** prints and stickers add up fast. Set a number before you walk in.
- **Food:** convention-center food is pricey. Bring snacks (most venues allow them) or eat nearby.
- **Cash:** card readers are common, but cash still speeds up small purchases and sometimes gets a better price.

## Etiquette

- **Cosplay is not consent.** Always ask before taking a photo of a cosplayer.
- **Don't block aisles** for photos. Step to the side.
- **Shower, sleep, eat.** The old con-goer rule, sometimes called the 6-2-1 rule (six hours of sleep, two meals, one shower a day), exists for a reason.
